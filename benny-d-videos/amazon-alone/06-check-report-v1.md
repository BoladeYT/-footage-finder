# CHECK SUMMARY: Amazon Alone

This check ran in the same chat that wrote the script, so it isn't independent. Your own read is the only true first listen.

## Is it ready?
Almost. Two small things need fixing first. Both are about getting a real-world detail exactly right.

## What needs fixing
1. **The fever advice at the end has a time limit we can't back up (B16).** The script says "for weeks after you get home, any fever means getting checked by a doctor". The NHS says malaria symptoms can appear months after travel, rarely even years, and advises getting help for symptoms within a year of visiting a malaria area.
   Why it matters: a viewer could act on this. "For weeks" might make someone ignore a fever two months later.
   Recommended fix: Research adds the correct time window to the brief, then the line is changed to match it, or the "for weeks" limit is simply removed.
2. **The snakebite number describes the wrong group of people (B07).** The script says "nearly nine in ten snakebite patients" at a hospital in the region were bitten on the leg or foot. The study only counted people bitten by pit vipers, the region's main venomous snakes, not all snakebite patients.
   Why it matters: it's a small stretch of a real figure, and a careful viewer or fact-checker could catch it.
   Recommended fix: say who the figure is about, for example people bitten by pit vipers, or make the line less specific.

## Optional improvements
A. **"The lodge's name" (B05).** "You" shout it, but the lodge is never named, so the listener has nothing to picture. · Why: a small snag on first listen. · Suggested fix: name the lodge in the opening, or change what "you" shout.
B. **The last minute is heavy (B16).** It packs four real-world points into a short stretch, and the sentence about leptospirosis is long to hear. · Why: the ending's key lesson could get lost in the list. · Suggested fix: split the leptospirosis sentence, or trim one point.
C. **The snakebite advice sounds a little like a lecture (B07).** "The World Health Organization says…" is the one place the story briefly sounds like a lesson. · Why: it's well placed, just a touch stiff aloud. · Suggested fix: keep the facts and make the delivery more natural.
D. **"Water runs downhill. Small streams run into bigger ones." (B10).** This is everyday physics, not something Research checked. · Why: almost certainly harmless, but it isn't backed by the brief. · Suggested fix: leave it, or have Research add a source.

## What's working well
- The snapped saplings: you see them snapped in the morning, and finding them again is the story's strongest moment.
- The jaguar never comes, and the one real animal danger, the snake, stays at its true size.
- The "so close" ending plays fair. The shouting on night 1 and the search starting next morning explain why nobody heard you.
- The day-3 decision to drink from the stream is a real, honest trade-off.
