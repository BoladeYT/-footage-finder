# COLD-READ NOTES: Amazon Alone (check v1)

Procedure note: written from the cleaned script only (05-script-deslopped-v1.md), before opening the Story Map, the Research Brief, the Bible sections or the Log. This check runs in the same conversation that produced the script, so this is a procedure, not a true first listen.

- Opening (B01–B03) is clear on a single listen: the place (a river lodge a few hours by boat from Manaus), who "you" are (a visitor, alone), what you carry (phone, half-full bottle, no lighter, no knife), and the friction (the noise, the run). "You" are lost and alone by the end of B03. The promise lands early.
- B05: "the lodge's name" — the lodge is never named, so this phrase points at something the listener doesn't have. Minor.
- B07: the World Health Organization passage arrives straight after the near-miss. It is placed in the pause, but "The World Health Organization says…" reads slightly stiff aloud; it is the one place the story briefly sounds like a lecture.
- B09: the circle discovery lands well. The snapped saplings set up in B06 pay off cleanly. The study explanation is short and placed at the right moment.
- B10: the reasoning is easy to follow: stop/stay advice, why it doesn't fully fit, then the "water runs downhill" method. The trade-off is clear without menu narration.
- B11: "You don't make that mistake twice." reads as mild narrator foreshadowing; which mistake is meant (being over-sure) is clear enough.
- B12–B15: forward motion and relief work; the "one bend" reveal and "less than a kilometre" land. The timing of the search (first light on day 2) answers why nobody heard you.
- B16 is dense: four separate real-world points in a short space, and the leptospirosis sentence is long for the ear. Followable, but it's the heaviest stretch of information in the script.
- Lines that sound like real-world instructions: B07 (snakebite first aid), B08 (water can't be judged by sight; no fire to boil), B10 and B16 (stop and stay findable; follow water if you must move), B16 (get any fever checked). All need source checks.
- Machine-tell recurrence: a few "Not X. Y." shapes ("Not a bird. Not a monkey."; "This isn't carelessness. It's how people work."; "The jungle didn't trap you. Your senses did."), and several one-line paragraph turns. Each works where it is; no run reads templated.
